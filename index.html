Mechanisms you'll want in the dumpster

Wet wood: moisture delays ignition (pine, 35 kW/m²)

Water has to boil off before the surface can pass 100 °C. That plateau pushes ignition back, and a soaked pallet only steams for a while.

A PET bottle wall (0.3 mm), with and without dripping

Held flat in a pan, the thin wall ignites in seconds. Held vertical, the melt runs off before it can make enough fuel vapour, which is why bottles sag and drip in a fire instead of flaring. In the game, those drips should become a pool on the dumpster floor.

What needs tuning before the 3‑D layers

    What already holds up

      Method

      • Each object face is a stack of thin layers. Conduction is implicit, with a radiating, convecting surface and an insulated back face, as with cone backing board. Water boils off at 100 °C, and PET melts at 250 °C, both by enthalpy clamp.
      • Arrhenius kinetics are pinned to each material's TGA peak temperature, and the gas leaves instantly. Wood leaves char in place, PU foam collapses into a liquid polyol pool, and PET leaves a small dispersed residue.
      • Ignition happens when fuel vapour flux holds above a critical value for a short delay. After that, the flame adds heat to the surface. HRR = vapour flux × effective heat of combustion, using FSRI's values.
      • Verification: on an inert semi-infinite solid with no losses, the surface temperature stays within of the analytic √t solution. Energy closes as well: total heat released equals each sample's fuel load.
      • Calibration used a bounded coordinate search on four knobs per material (critical vapour flux, flame heat flux, and two kinetic or heat-of-reaction terms), fit to the 25 and 50 kW/m² data only.
      Sources
      • FSRI Materials & Products Database: Wood Stud, Overstuffed Chair PU Foam, PET, Corrugated Cardboard, Pine Siding
      • NIST TN 2043: cone calorimetry of flexible PU foam at 35 kW/m²
      • White & Dietenberger: southern pine at 50 kW/m² · Timbers at 35 kW/m² (WIT Transactions)
      • Spearpoint & Quintiere (2001): piloted ignition of wood, minimum flux ≈ 10 kW/m²
